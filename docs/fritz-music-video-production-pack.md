# Fritz Academy — Music Video Production Pack

Purpose: use remaining ElevenLabs credits efficiently after class while preserving Fritz character and curriculum canon. Songs are reusable curriculum anchors, not one song per lesson.

## Non-negotiable visual canon
- Cast: Captain Fritz, Bash, Tony, Nola, Rascal, Bear.
- Dogs remain dogs; no human student cast replacing them.
- No shoes on the dogs.
- Bash is the real/natural leader and Bear's younger but much larger brother.
- Tony thinks he is in charge; confident comic energy, never mean.
- Nola is the worrier; expressive but capable.
- Rascal is mischievous/troublemaking, not malicious.
- Bear is a semi-troublemaker who is often stopped by Bash.
- Captain Fritz is the mentor and should not dominate every shot.
- Maintain established character appearance from canonical Fritz assets. Bash accuracy is especially important. Bear should match his established lighter appearance.
- Academy world should look polished, warm, adventurous, colorful and cinematic—not preschool clip art and not a classroom worksheet.
- Alphabet blocks remain a recurring visual motif.

## Video production language
- Audience: beginner English learners, roughly elementary age; never babyish.
- Format target: 16:9 landscape master; safe central composition for later crops.
- Length target: 1:45–2:30 per reusable song unless musical structure needs otherwise.
- Visual rhythm: new meaningful action/shot every 3–6 seconds; repeat choruses with changed action rather than identical footage.
- On-screen words: large, clean, minimal; show the target word exactly when sung. Avoid dense lyric screens.
- Use visual meaning before translation: action, expression, object, color, number, family relationship, etc.
- Chorus should be memorable enough to recur across many lessons.
- No random wardrobe changes or character-model drift between shots.

## Priority 1 — Alphabet Adventure Song / Video rebuild
Status: existing song is liked and should be preserved/reused where possible. Video needs continuity-quality rebuild.

Curriculum purpose:
- A–Z letter-name fluency.
- Alphabet order.
- Recurring phonics support across Episodes 1–9 and later decoding review.

Must-have visuals:
- Physical alphabet blocks in the Academy world.
- Characters interact with blocks rather than floating letters only.
- Bash organizes/rebuilds a block path; Tony tries to direct; Rascal causes a harmless block mix-up; Bear almost joins him; Bash stops him; Nola notices a missing/wrong block; Captain Fritz provides the final mentor nudge.
- Letter shown on screen must match sung letter.
- End with a satisfying A–Z structure/build completed from the blocks.

Video concept:
The Music Box activates an Alphabet Builder course. Blocks illuminate in sequence as the dogs race through the Academy collecting and placing them. Small character comedy happens between letter groups, but the alphabet remains visually legible. Final chorus reveals the completed A–Z build and opens an Academy path.

## Priority 2 — Feelings Song: “How Do You Feel?”
Curriculum purpose:
- happy, sad, mad/angry, scared, tired, excited, okay.
- question/answer: “How do you feel?” “I feel ___.”
- useful from beginner introductions onward.

Song concept:
Call-and-response. Captain Fritz or group asks “How do you feel?” Different characters answer through small story moments. Nola can model worried/scared then okay; Rascal excited; Bear tired after following Rascal; Tony confident/excited; Bash calm/okay/happy. Emotions must be clear through dog posture, face and action.

Video concept:
A day at the Academy keeps changing. Each event causes a real emotion rather than characters posing beside emoji faces. Chorus brings everyone back together around the Music Box. On-screen target word appears briefly with each emotion.

Avoid:
- making Nola permanently fearful or helpless.
- angry characters threatening each other.
- generic emoji-only teaching.

## Priority 3 — Family Song: “This Is My Family”
Curriculum purpose:
- family, mother/mom, father/dad, brother, sister; later grandmother/grandfather.
- “This is my ___.” “Who is this?”

Canon/story opportunities:
- Bash and Bear are brothers. Bash is younger but much larger.
- Nola can have a sister.
- Rascal can have a brother and sister.
- Use a Family Day at the Academy so several dog families can appear naturally.
- All family members remain dog characters; no human family substitutions.

Video concept:
Family Day banners go up at the Academy. Each established character welcomes family members, giving visual context for the relationship word. Bash/Bear brother moment should be memorable and funny. End with a group family photo/building display.

## Priority 4 — Action Song: “Move With Fritz”
Curriculum purpose:
- run, walk, jump, sit, stand, turn, stop, clap/tap as appropriate for dog-world presentation.
- commands: “Run!” “Stop!” “Turn!”

Video concept:
An Academy obstacle course. Learner follows commands with the dogs. Bash demonstrates correctly, Tony tries to be course captain, Rascal goes early, Bear starts to follow Rascal and Bash redirects him, Nola checks the signs, Captain Fritz starts/stops rounds.

Important dog-world note:
Do not depict dogs clapping human hands. Use learner-facing prompts where a human child can clap, while dogs can tap paws, jump, turn, sit, stand, run and stop.

## Priority 5 — Colors Song: “Find the Color”
Curriculum purpose:
- red, blue, yellow, green, orange, purple, black, white, brown; color question “What color is it?”

Video concept:
The Academy Color Gallery loses its color lights. Characters restore rooms/doors by finding objects of the target color. Use color as part of an adventure puzzle rather than a sequence of static color cards.

## Priority 6 — Numbers Song: “Count the Clues”
Curriculum purpose:
- 1–10 first; later reusable extension to 20.
- “How many?” “There are ___.”

Video concept:
A clue hunt where the dogs count tracks, blocks, keys, lights and objects to unlock the Music Box. Counts must be visually exact—never show six objects while singing five.

## ElevenLabs production order after class
1. Alphabet Adventure — use existing liked song/audio if available; spend credits primarily on the improved video/voice needs rather than unnecessarily replacing a song people already like.
2. Feelings Song — high reuse and strong character performance.
3. Family Song — directly supports the Family Day arc and character canon.
4. Action Song — highly reusable and visually easy to make interactive.
5. Colors Song.
6. Numbers Song.

## Information to carry into any AI music prompt
Style: energetic, warm, catchy educational pop/adventure song; clear English diction; moderate tempo; strong repeatable chorus; elementary-age rather than toddler tone; short phrases suitable for A0/A1 learners; no slang that obscures meaning; target vocabulary repeated naturally; musical pauses where the learner can answer or act.

Voice direction:
- Clear international-English intelligibility is more important than vocal ornamentation.
- Keep consonants crisp and target words easy to hear.
- Avoid overly fast rap passages for beginner material.
- If character voices are used, maintain consistent casting across songs.

## Acceptance test for every finished song/video
1. Is every sung target word clearly audible?
2. Does the visual show the correct meaning at that moment?
3. Are the established Fritz characters on-model and behaving in-role?
4. Are the dogs shoe-free and anatomically/behaviorally consistent with the Fritz world?
5. Does the video feel like part of the Academy adventure rather than an isolated lesson?
6. Can the song be reused in multiple episodes without feeling tied to one exact lesson?
7. Are text, object counts, letters and colors factually synchronized with the audio?
8. Would a child plausibly want to replay it even if nobody called it schoolwork?
